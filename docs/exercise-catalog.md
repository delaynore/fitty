# Exercise catalog update

## Built-in catalog

Fitty ships with a few dozen common exercises for home and gym workouts, including running and timed exercises. The whole catalog is available offline.

Settings opens the catalog for browsing. The lifter can search and open an exercise card to read its characteristics and instructions for performing the movement.

The lifter searches by name and can filter by muscle group and available equipment. The equipment filter covers both home and gym use. Each built-in exercise has a technical difficulty level: beginner, intermediate, or advanced.

The exercise card distinguishes the primary muscle group from assisting muscle groups. When filtering by a muscle group, the lifter chooses its role. For example, filtering for biceps as the primary group finds curls. Filtering for biceps as an assisting group finds pulling exercises that list biceps in that role.

## Existing programs

After an app update, exercises entered as text retain their original names and the weight-and-repetitions type. The lifter can choose a catalog exercise manually in the training day editor. For example, an entry called “Press” stays “Press” until the lifter chooses a specific variation. Past workout records retain the names and results recorded at the time.

## Choosing an exercise

In the training day editor, **Add exercise** opens the catalog for selection. Tapping a name opens the exercise card. Tapping **+** adds the exercise and immediately returns to the day being edited. The add-to-day action belongs to this selection screen.

When the lifter adds an exercise to a training day, they search the exercise catalog. If the exercise is missing, **Create exercise** opens a separate creation screen with the search text filled in as the name. The name and exercise type are required. The lifter can fill in muscles, equipment, and difficulty later. Saving adds the exercise to the day being edited and makes it available in the catalog for future use.

An exercise type determines what the lifter can record. A training day can give the exercise a target. A workout session records what the lifter actually completed. The same exercise can have different targets on different training days.

Fitty supports four exercise types:

- Weight and repetitions
- Repetitions
- Duration
- Distance

For weighted pull-ups, use weight and repetitions. The recorded weight is the extra load the lifter attached. For ordinary pull-ups, use repetitions.

## Timing a set

For a duration exercise, the lifter starts the exercise timer when they begin the set. After recording the result, the lifter can start the rest timer separately.

When the set reaches its target duration, Fitty signals with sound and vibration and continues timing, including while the screen is locked. Pressing **Stop** immediately records the full elapsed time as a logged set. For a 40-second target, pressing **Stop** at 47 seconds records 47 seconds in history. The lifter can edit that set afterward.

## Training day editor

Each exercise starts as a compact row showing its name and target, for example “Plank · 3 × 40 s”. Tapping the row expands the target fields directly below the summary. Tapping it again collapses the fields. The drag handle on the right changes the exercise order.

The lifter can drag exercises into the desired order while creating or editing a training day. A workout starts in that order. During a workout the lifter can jump to any exercise and log sets in any sequence.

## Finding a replacement

Show recommended replacements first. These are manually selected pairs with similar movements and may use different equipment. A second section shows other exercises with the same primary muscle group and excludes those already shown as recommended replacements.

During a workout, the lifter can replace an exercise for that session, for example when a machine is out of order. Future workouts use the exercise in the program. To change future workouts, the lifter edits the training day.

A replacement with the same exercise type keeps compatible targets. For example, replacing a barbell press with a dumbbell press keeps the target of three sets of eight repetitions. The lifter enters the weight used for the dumbbells. When the exercise type changes, only the target number of sets carries over.

Replacing an exercise after logging a set for it is outside the scope of this update.
