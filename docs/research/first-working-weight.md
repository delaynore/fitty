# Choosing a first working weight

For a lifter trying an unfamiliar exercise, Fitty could use a light trial set to find a weight they can lift for the training day's target repetitions with good technique and a few repetitions left. Someone who already knows a working weight for the same exercise and equipment can enter it directly. This is a proposed workflow for healthy adults doing resistance exercises measured in weight and repetitions.

## What the evidence supports

- **Novices can pick a starting point.** Glass and Stanton asked 30 young novice lifters to choose their own loads on five exercises and later measured their one-repetition maximum (1RM) on each. Mean selections ranged from 42% to 57% of 1RM across exercises and groups. The study measured what participants chose. It did not test which first weight produced the best training outcomes. [Glass and Stanton, 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15142014/)
- **Effort helps interpret the rep count.** Repetitions in reserve (RIR) estimates how many more repetitions a lifter could complete before reaching failure. The American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM) suggests about 2–3 RIR as one way to achieve sufficient effort. Training to failure showed no consistent advantage across outcomes. The stand's finding that heavier loads (at least 80% of 1RM) enhance maximal strength comes from programs lasting at least six weeks. It cannot establish the right weight for someone's first set. [ACSM position stand, 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/)
- **Treat the lifter's answer as approximate.** In a study of 58 trained and untrained adults performing curls, triceps pushdowns and seated rows, estimates of remaining repetitions improved closer to failure and in later sets. Training experience had no significant effect on accuracy in that study. This supports asking a simple effort question after the trial and letting the lifter revise their answer. [Remmert and colleagues, 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37036795/)
- **Percentages need a personal reference.** A 1RM is the heaviest weight the individual can lift once on a particular exercise. At the same percentage of their 1RM, study participants could generally do more repetitions on a back squat than a bench press or arm curl. A multi-repetition maximum can also estimate 1RM. In bench press and leg press tests, predictions from a five-repetition maximum fit the measured 1RM better than predictions from a ten- or twenty-repetition maximum. All those tests used sets taken to failure. An ordinary logged set of five easy repetitions provides different information. [Shimano and colleagues, 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17194239/) · [Reynolds and colleagues, 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16937972/)

## How Fitty could apply this

1. Use the training day's recommended repetitions when available. If the lifter has no target, offer a simple starting range such as 8–12 repetitions or let them enter their own. Public guidance for general strengthening uses 8–12 repetitions and advises building up gradually. [Fitty workout workflow](../mvp.md#workout-workflow) · [NHS strength guidance](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/exercise/how-to-improve-strength-flexibility/)
2. For an unfamiliar exercise, ask the lifter to select a light weight they can control. If the lightest available bar is already too heavy, suggest a lighter implement or a suitable alternative. For a familiar exercise, a recent set on the same equipment at a similar rep count gives the lifter a starting reference. [Mayo Clinic weight-training guidance](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/fitness/in-depth/weight-training/art-20045842)
3. Ask how the last repetition felt. If the lifter reaches the target and could do many more, suggest the next comfortable equipment increment after resting. If they reach the target with about two or three good repetitions left, the set can count as their first working set. If technique breaks down, they miss the lower end of the target or they have to strain for the last rep, suggest less weight for the next set. An unsure lifter can keep a manageable weight and reassess on the next set.
4. Record the repetitions and weight actually completed. For example, on a seated row with a target of ten, 15 kg for ten may feel easy. If 20 kg for ten leaves about two more good repetitions, the lifter can log 20 kg for ten as their first working set. These example loads, the effort prompt and the size of each adjustment are product choices. A universal starting weight or fixed kilogram increment does not follow from the studies above. [Fitty workout records](../mvp.md#workout-records)

This flow assumes that adding external weight makes the exercise harder. On an assisted pull-up, adding assistance makes the exercise easier. A repetition-only exercise needs no weight prompt. [Fitty exercise types](exercise-tracking-types.md)
