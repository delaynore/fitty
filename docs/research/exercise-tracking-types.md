# Exercise types and the fields in a logged set

Fitty defines an exercise type by what the lifter records in one set. Hevy documents eight types that fit this definition. The sample values below illustrate what each entry would mean in workout history. [Fitty glossary](../../GLOSSARY.md#language) · [Hevy exercise types](https://www.hevyapp.com/features/custom-exercises/)

| Fields in one set | Exercise | Example entry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weight and repetitions | Bench press | 60 kg × 8 |
| Repetitions | Pull-up | 8 reps |
| Added weight and repetitions | Weighted pull-up | 8 reps with 10 kg added |
| Assistance and repetitions | Machine-assisted pull-up | 8 reps with 20 kg of assistance |
| Duration | Plank | 45 s |
| Weight and duration | Weighted wall sit | 10 kg for 45 s |
| Distance and duration | Rowing machine | 500 m in 2 min 10 s |
| Weight and distance | Suitcase carry | 24 kg for 30 m |

The three pull-up entries show why the weight label matters. At the same bodyweight and rep count, lowering machine assistance from 20 kg to 15 kg makes the set harder. Adding another 5 kg to a belt also makes it harder. A plain pull-up only needs a rep count. [Hevy guide to bodyweight exercises](https://help.hevyapp.com/hc/en-us/articles/44098622782487-Bodyweight-Exercises-in-Hevy-Bodyweight-vs-Assisted-vs-Weighted)

For Fitty, the same field labels could follow an exercise from the training day into the workout session and history. A timed hold needs only its duration. For rowing, recording distance as well lets the lifter compare pace across intervals. Strong likewise compares different measures for different exercise categories, including repetitions, time and distance.
